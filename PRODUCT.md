# Product

## Register

**Brand:** Lalit Sharma — AI Developer & AI Systems Engineer

## Users

Recruiters, hiring managers, engineering leads, and technical reviewers evaluating Lalit Sharma for AI Developer, Junior AI/ML Engineer, Generative AI Engineer, and AI Systems Engineering roles.

Recruiters need to understand his role fit, strongest projects, measurable outcomes, and contact details quickly. Technical reviewers need enough implementation detail to evaluate his experience with machine learning, LLM applications, RAG, autonomous agents, computer vision, backend engineering, and AI-powered applications.

## Product Purpose

Present Lalit Sharma as an AI developer who builds practical, end-to-end intelligent systems, from model experimentation and data processing to LLM integration, backend APIs, orchestration, and application delivery.

The portfolio should help visitors:

- Identify the three strongest projects quickly.
- Understand each project's purpose, technical architecture, individual contributions, and verified outcomes.
- Explore work across Generative AI, autonomous agents, RAG and document intelligence, AI data analytics, computer vision, and voice-based AI applications.
- Access the resume and contact information without navigating through the entire experience.
- Explore technical depth through interactive project details and demonstrations where available.

Success means a recruiter can understand Lalit's profile and find the next step within 60–90 seconds, while technical visitors can explore the implementation details without losing the portfolio's distinctive interactive experience.

## Brand Personality

Technical, cinematic, precise, and engineering-led.

The experience should communicate practical problem-solving and technical curiosity. Visual effects should reinforce the narrative of building intelligent systems without compromising readability, performance, accessibility, or usability.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic developer templates, interchangeable SaaS card grids, decorative animation without purpose, terminal styling without technical meaning, unsupported performance claims, and portfolio experiences that hide important evidence behind lengthy introductions or fragile visual effects.

Avoid presenting experimental, in-progress, or unverified work as a completed production system.

## Design Principles

- **Lead with proof:** Prioritize meaningful projects, implementation details, and verifiable outcomes over broad claims.
- **Show engineering depth:** Explain architecture, model integration, data flows, tools, trade-offs, and deployment where relevant.
- **Earn the spectacle:** Use expressive motion to support the narrative and technical identity.
- **Offer two speeds:** Give recruiters a quick route to projects, resume, and contact information while allowing technical peers to explore deeper details.
- **Keep project status honest:** Clearly distinguish completed projects, working prototypes, contributions, and work in progress.
- **Degrade with intent:** Preserve complete content and navigation when WebGL, advanced animation, or other resource-intensive effects are unavailable.
- **Keep contact close:** Make resume access and contact navigation easy to discover.
- **Protect performance:** Keep animations responsive, avoid blocking access to content, and support integrated-GPU and lower-capability devices.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for contrast, keyboard navigation, focus visibility, landmarks, and accessible interaction.

Respect reduced-motion preferences. Avoid forced large-scale motion in reduced-motion or degraded modes. Ensure all project information remains available without WebGL, and support touchscreens, keyboard-only navigation, small viewports, and integrated-GPU devices without losing essential functionality.

All essential content must remain reachable through normal scrolling, and section transitions must never prevent users from viewing project cards or accessing navigation.
